DIFFICULTY

Sets the GWAS sample size N. LD is estimated from the same samples, so a smaller N means fainter peaks and noisier LD.

HOW IT WORKS
  • The region. Each world is a stretch of genome with a few dozen variants. Nearby variants are inherited together (linkage disequilibrium, LD), so they're correlated: strongly when close, weakly when far apart, with patches where recombination broke things up. Variants are built from overlapping haplotype segments plus a chain along the region, so the LD decays with distance and is patchy, like real data.
  • The phenotype. A few variants are causal, each explaining 1–4% of the phenotype's variance, with random signs. The rest do nothing.
  • What you see. The GWAS: −log10 p from testing each variant on its own. And the LD (r²) between variants, estimated from the same N samples, so it carries sampling noise of about 1/√N.
  • Your rival. STEP-BOT runs a stepwise conditional analysis: take the strongest variant, refit conditioning on it, and repeat while the best remaining one passes p < 0.05 / variants.
  • Hints. Toggle on functional annotations (missense, promoter, enhancer) for every variant. Causal variants are more likely to carry one, as in real data, but most annotated variants are still bystanders. Using hints in a world costs 20% of its coins. Methods like PolyFun use annotations in the same way.
  • Story mode. Six worlds shaped like real loci, with what fine-mapping and follow-up work actually found. The data are simulated to echo each story; the LD and effect sizes aren't the real ones.
  • Scoring. Each world is worth 100 coins: twice the number you got right, divided by your picks plus the true causal variants (the F1 score). A wrong pick and a missed causal variant cost the same.
  • Class mode. The settings and a game code go in the link, so everyone who opens it plays the same worlds.

WHY IS THIS HARD?
  • A variant in strong LD with a causal one carries almost the same signal, so the tallest peak is often a bystander.
  • Two causal variants in LD can add up and make a bystander between them look strongest, or cancel out when their effects have opposite signs.
  • LD estimated from a few hundred samples is noisy: variants can look linked, or unlinked, by chance.
  • Conditioning on one causal variant makes its neighbours' signals vanish. Conditional analysis and fine-mapping methods (SuSiE, FINEMAP) build on that, but none of them can separate variants in near-perfect LD. That needs more samples, other populations, or functional data.
